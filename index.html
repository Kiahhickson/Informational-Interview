Assessment 3 - Part 1 ·  Informational Interview

Preparing for the next stage of leadership

As I approach the end of my Bachelor of Business, I wanted to better understand what it will take to move from principal-level digital work into broader Director and executive leadership.

I spoke with John Oberhardt, Executive Recruiter at Oberhardt Consulting, former General Manager Operations at Redland City Council and a long-time mentor, to better understand what executive readiness looks like and what I should focus on next.

Read the full interview transcript →

01The interviewee

Why John?

A mentor who has seen my development over time and now sees executive recruitment from the other side of the table.

Interviewee

John Oberhardt

Executive Recruiter · Oberhardt Consulting

John works in public-sector executive recruitment, specialising in senior and executive appointments. His role gives him direct insight into how candidates are assessed, what organisations look for in senior leaders, and what organisations look for when appointing Directors and executives. Previously, as General Manager Operations at Redland City Council, he led large operational portfolios and developed extensive experience in people leadership, organisational strategy and public administration.

Why I chose him

I chose John because he has seen my professional development from two very different perspectives. As General Manager Operations at Redland City Council, he supported my growth and encouraged me to begin university while working full-time. Now, as an Executive Recruiter with Oberhardt Consulting, he assesses senior public-sector candidates and understands what organisations look for when appointing leaders.

Because John knew me earlier in my career and now works directly in executive recruitment, his perspective was particularly relevant to my goal of moving from principal-level digital work into broader Director and executive leadership in the public sector.

What I wanted to learn
  1. What does executive readiness actually look like?
  2. What experience should I prioritise next?
  3. Do I need more study, or more leadership experience?
02Trends

Digital leadership is getting broader

John said digital skills are becoming more important at senior levels, but “digital” now means much more than systems or social media.

01

Digital means more than technology

Depending on the organisation, digital can include data, customer experience, communications, transformation, AI and how organisations use technology to work differently and improve services.

02

Senior leaders need strategic breadth

As people progress, less of their value comes from being operationally hands-on and more from judgement, strategy and connecting different parts of the organisation.

03

AI is changing recruitment

With more applications being written or polished using AI, John said recruiters increasingly need to dig deeper to understand what someone has actually done, how they think and whether the person they meet matches the application.

03Insights

What makes someone ready to lead?

The interview shifted my thinking away from “what else should I learn?” toward “how do I become broader as a leader?”

01

Strategic thinking

Seeing beyond the immediate task and understanding organisational impact.

02

People leadership

Supporting, developing and managing people with different strengths.

03

Self-awareness

Knowing where I am strong and where I need others around me.

04

Relationships

Building credibility across teams, peers and senior decision-makers.

05

Judgement

Making balanced decisions without needing to be the expert in every discipline.

Big shift in thinking

The interview helped me realise that the thing I need most next probably isn’t another course. It’s broader leadership experience.

04Feedback

What John sees in me

Because John knew me earlier in my career, he could reflect on how my strengths have changed over time.

01

Technical growth

John said my skills and the quality of my work have continued to strengthen.

02

Strategic mindset

I increasingly look beyond delivery and try to understand the bigger organisational picture.

03

Leadership style

John described my approach as supportive, genuine and balanced.

04

Personal growth

John felt I had matured considerably since we first worked together, particularly in how I approach work, people and my longer-term career.

05Advice

Be deliberate about the next move

John’s advice was not to “shotgun” applications, but to choose opportunities where my genuine strengths align with what the organisation actually needs.

  1. Target opportunities rather than applying broadly.
  2. Research the organisation and decision-makers.
  3. Be honest about the type of leader I am.
  4. Don’t try to become the person I think an organisation wants just to win the job.
  5. Keep saying yes to stretch opportunities.
  6. Build experience outside my immediate area of expertise.

Stretch experience already in motion

John specifically highlighted my Queensland Day secondment as a strong example of experience working outside my usual role that I should use in future applications.

He also saw Army Reserve service as a useful point of difference that could demonstrate service, discipline, teamwork and leadership.

06Development

Study or experience?

One of the clearest outcomes from the conversation was permission not to immediately chase another qualification.

MBA now?

Not yet.

MBA later?

Potentially, especially once I am in a broader leadership role.

Priority now

Gain more experience leading people, managing budgets and resources, and making strategic decisions.

07Resources

Where to learn next

John suggested I pause formal study for now and keep developing through practical leadership resources, professional networks and self-awareness tools.

01

Leadership learning

Once university finishes, keep learning in a less formal way through leadership books, podcasts and people whose leadership style I respect.

02

Professional networks

Research public-sector leadership and professional associations, and rebuild the kind of active industry network I previously had in technology.

03

Self-assessment

Use leadership or psychometric tools to better understand my strengths, blind spots and the kinds of people I need around me.

08Assignments

Learn by doing

John’s career reinforced the value of stretch assignments: saying yes to acting opportunities, unfamiliar portfolios and problems that sit outside a person’s original specialty.

01

Stretch roles

Pursue acting roles, secondments and cross-functional work that gives me more experience leading people and working outside my current specialty.

02

Problem-solving assignments

Volunteer for complex work that requires me to coordinate people, make decisions and solve problems beyond digital communications.

03

Tell the story

Use experiences such as the Queensland Day secondment and future Army Reserve service as evidence of leadership, teamwork, adaptability and contribution.

09Networking

My biggest gap

Earlier in my tech career, networking happened naturally. In government, I have relied much more on relationships built through work.

John encouraged me to deliberately build professional relationships outside my immediate workplace, particularly through public-sector events, professional associations and leadership networks.

Next step

Build more professional connections beyond my immediate network, particularly with senior digital, transformation and public-sector leaders.

10Reflection
I entered the interview asking
“What else do I need to add?”

Another qualification? More technical expertise? Another credential?

→
I left asking
“How do I become broader as a leader?”

I already have a strong technical base, substantial professional experience and, shortly, a completed business degree. My next stage is about people, strategy, relationships, self-awareness and judgement.

That comment stayed with me because it is probably true. I am very driven and, once I know where I want to go, I tend to want to get there quickly. That has helped me achieve a lot, but it can also make me impatient with parts of career development that simply take time.

I didn’t take John’s advice as “slow down”. I took it as a reminder that I can keep moving while still allowing myself time to build the experience I need.

11Outcomes

Positive outcomes from the interview

The conversation did more than provide career advice. It strengthened an existing mentoring relationship and created a clear pathway for continued support.

Ongoing mentoring

John encouraged me to keep him updated after graduation and said he would continue to help and guide me where he can. This gives me someone experienced I can keep checking in with as I consider future opportunities.

A clearer development focus

The discussion confirmed that my immediate priority is not another qualification. It is building more experience leading people, strengthening my professional network and improving my self-awareness before deciding whether postgraduate study adds value.

12Next steps

What changes now?

The long-term goal has not changed. The pathway is simply clearer.

1

Lead

Target roles with more responsibility for leading people and work, including strategy and resources.

2

Connect

Deliberately expand my network beyond my current workplace.

3

Understand myself

Complete a leadership or psychometric assessment and use the results to identify the areas I should work on next.
