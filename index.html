Assessment 3 - Part 1 ·  Informational Interview

Preparing for the next stage of leadership

As I approach the end of my Bachelor of Business, I wanted to better understand what it will take to move from principal-level digital work into broader Director and executive leadership.

I spoke with John Oberhardt — Executive Recruiter at Oberhardt Consulting, former General Manager Operations at Redland City Council, and a long-time mentor — to better understand what executive readiness looks like and what I should deliberately develop next.

01The interviewee

Why John?

A mentor who has seen my development over time — and now sees executive recruitment from the other side of the table.

Interviewee

John Oberhardt

Executive Recruiter · Oberhardt Consulting

John works in public-sector executive recruitment, specialising in senior and executive appointments. His role gives him direct insight into how candidates are assessed, what organisations look for in senior leaders, and the capabilities that distinguish strong applicants at Director and executive level. Previously, as General Manager Operations at Redland City Council, he led large operational portfolios and developed extensive experience in people leadership, organisational strategy and public administration.

Why I chose him

I chose John because he has seen my professional development from two very different perspectives. As General Manager Operations at Redland City Council, he supported my growth and encouraged me to begin university while working full-time. Now, as an Executive Recruiter with Oberhardt Consulting, he assesses senior public-sector candidates and understands what organisations look for when appointing leaders.

This combination of knowing my earlier career and now working directly with executive recruitment made his perspective particularly relevant to my goal of progressing from principal-level digital work into broader Director and executive leadership in the public sector.

What I wanted to learn
  1. What does executive readiness actually look like?
  2. What experience should I prioritise next?
  3. Do I need more study — or more leadership experience?
02Trends

Digital leadership is getting broader

John’s view was that digital capability is becoming more important at senior levels — but “digital” now means much more than systems or social media.

01

Digital means more than technology

Depending on the organisation, digital can include data, customer experience, communications, transformation, AI and the broader future of technology.

02

Senior leaders need strategic breadth

As people progress, less of their value comes from being operationally hands-on and more from judgement, strategy and connecting different parts of the organisation.

03

AI is changing recruitment

With more applications being AI-assisted, recruiters increasingly need to look beneath polished wording to understand what candidates have genuinely done and who they really are.

03Insights

What makes someone ready to lead?

The interview shifted my thinking away from “what else should I learn?” toward “how do I become broader as a leader?”

01

Strategic thinking

Seeing beyond the immediate task and understanding organisational impact.

02

People leadership

Supporting, developing and managing people with different strengths.

03

Self-awareness

Knowing where I am strong and where I need others around me.

04

Relationships

Building credibility across teams, peers and senior decision-makers.

05

Judgement

Making balanced decisions without needing to be the expert in every discipline.

Big shift in thinking

I had been asking what additional qualification or technical capability I needed. The interview helped me recognise that my next stage of growth is more likely to come from broader leadership experience.

04Feedback

What John sees in me

Because John knew me earlier in my career, he could reflect on how my strengths have changed over time.

01

Technical growth

My capability and the quality of my work have continued to strengthen.

02

Strategic mindset

I increasingly look beyond delivery and try to understand the bigger organisational picture.

03

Leadership style

John described my approach as supportive, genuine and balanced.

04

Maturity

He identified personal maturity as one of the biggest areas of growth since my time at Council.

05Advice

Be deliberate about the next move

John’s advice was not to “shotgun” applications, but to choose opportunities where my genuine strengths align with what the organisation actually needs.

  1. Target opportunities rather than applying broadly.
  2. Research the organisation and decision-makers.
  3. Be honest about the type of leader I am.
  4. Do not reshape my personal brand just to fit a vacancy.
  5. Keep saying yes to stretch opportunities.
  6. Build experience outside my immediate area of expertise.

Stretch experience already in motion

John specifically highlighted my Queensland Day secondment as a strong example of the kind of cross-organisational experience I should use in future applications.

He also saw Army Reserve service as a useful point of difference that could demonstrate service, discipline, teamwork and leadership.

06Development

Study or experience?

One of the clearest outcomes from the conversation was permission not to immediately chase another qualification.

MBA now?

Not yet.

MBA later?

Potentially — especially once I am in a broader leadership role.

Priority now

Gain formal leadership responsibility and experience managing people, budgets, resources and strategy.

07Resources

Where to learn next

John’s advice was to pause formal study for now, but keep developing through practical leadership resources, professional networks and self-awareness tools.

01

Leadership learning

Use the time freed up after university to read leadership books and listen to podcasts that broaden my thinking beyond technical expertise.

02

Professional networks

Research public-sector leadership and professional associations, and rebuild the kind of active industry network I previously had in technology.

03

Self-assessment

Use leadership or psychometric profiling as a tool to better understand strengths, blind spots and how I can build complementary capability around me.

08Assignments

Learn by doing

John’s career reinforced the value of stretch assignments: saying yes to acting opportunities, unfamiliar portfolios and problems that sit outside a person’s original specialty.

01

Stretch roles

Pursue acting, secondment and cross-functional opportunities that build broader organisational experience and formal leadership responsibility.

02

Problem-solving assignments

Volunteer for complex work that requires me to coordinate people, make decisions and solve problems beyond my immediate digital communications remit.

03

Tell the story

Use experiences such as the Queensland Day secondment and future Army Reserve service as evidence of leadership, teamwork, adaptability and contribution.

09Networking

My biggest gap

Earlier in my tech career, networking happened naturally. In government, I have relied much more on relationships built through work.

John encouraged me to deliberately build professional relationships outside my immediate workplace, particularly through public-sector and leadership networks.

Next step

Build more weak ties with senior digital, transformation and public-sector leaders.

10Reflection
I entered the interview asking
“What else do I need to add?”

Another qualification? More technical expertise? Another credential?

→
I left asking
“How do I become broader as a leader?”

I already have a strong technical base, substantial professional experience and, shortly, a completed business degree. My next stage is about people, strategy, relationships, self-awareness and organisational judgement.

That comment stayed with me. Ambition has been one of the strongest drivers in my career, but it can also make me impatient with development that simply takes time. John’s advice was not to slow down or stop pursuing opportunities, but to recognise that some parts of leadership readiness are accumulated through experience.

11Outcomes

Positive outcomes from the interview

The conversation did more than provide career advice — it strengthened an existing mentoring relationship and created a clear pathway for continued support.

Ongoing mentoring

John encouraged me to keep him updated after graduation and said he would continue to help and guide me where he can. This gives me an experienced external sounding board as I consider future leadership opportunities.

A clearer development focus

The discussion confirmed that my immediate priority is not another qualification. It is building formal leadership experience, strengthening professional networks and improving self-awareness before deciding whether postgraduate study adds value.

12Next steps

What changes now?

The long-term goal has not changed. The pathway is simply clearer.

1

Lead

Target roles with greater formal responsibility for people, strategy and resources.

2

Connect

Deliberately expand my network beyond my current workplace.

3

Understand myself

Complete a leadership or psychometric assessment and use the results to identify development priorities.

The pathway
  1. Digital specialist
  2. Broader leadership
  3. Management responsibility
  4. Director
  5. Executive digital leadership
